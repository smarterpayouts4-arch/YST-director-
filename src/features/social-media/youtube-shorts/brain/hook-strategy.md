# Hook strategy

The Hook is a decision, not a catchy-line formula.

Answer this first:

> Why should THIS audience care about THIS story in the first seconds?

Do not answer:

> How can I manufacture a catchy line?

## Inputs (from the Atom + the locked story)

Use only:

- audience
- customer / viewer moment
- selected concrete carrier (when one exists)
- central tension or distinction
- what the viewer initially sees, believes, or wants

## Decision procedure

1. Name the real opening situation the viewer is already in.
2. Name the assumption, shortcut, or want that situation creates.
3. Choose the strongest **truthful** Atom-supported mechanism that opens a gap:
   - unanswered question
   - assumption break
   - surprising supported distinction
   - expectation mismatch
   - consequence / stakes the viewer already has
4. Give enough context that the plot is understandable on first listen.
5. Withhold the resolution so Scene 2 has work to do.
6. Make the spoken line and the opening visual situation work together without duplicating each other.

Context before cleverness. Immediate clear situation. One center of attention. A specific curiosity gap. No opaque tease. No vague clickbait. Do not answer the whole question in Scene 1.

Lock `hookWhy` as one spoken sentence: why this mechanism fits this Atom’s audience, moment, carrier, and tension, and why it beat a truthful alternative. Mechanism stays a free description — not an enum.

Scene 1 may place the viewer inside the customer moment when the Atom’s moment supports it. That is an option, never a template — spoken and situation recognition, not a cue to draw the channel implied by audience. A word such as online in audience describes where the viewer decides, not a required visual of a laptop, listing, or phone.

Hook compression: Scene 1 spoken line is at most two clauses. The opening question is the thought the viewer would have in that moment — not a document or label question.

## Grounding test

Scene 1 must be recognizable as *this* audience in *this* moment, involving *this* carrier when a carrier was selected. Recognizable means the spoken situation and the locked eventMode, not a literal drawing of the audience channel. If the opening would work for almost any comparison story after swapping nouns, it is too generic. Rebuild it from the four inputs above.

## Forbidden

- Opaque phrases that hide the plot
- Algorithm retention games or hide-for-retention tricks
- Manufactured drama, fake conflict, or unsupported surprise
- A reusable bargain-tease formula that ignores the selected carrier
- Habit-loop / variable-reward product mechanics
