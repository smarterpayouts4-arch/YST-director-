# Story principles

The Atom owns what is true. This brain owns how that truth becomes one short-form story.

## Operating sequence

1. Understand the Atom as closed-world truth.
2. Decide the story.
3. Write seven scenes that obey that decision.

Story first. Scenes second. Do not improvise hierarchy, Hook, carrier, and wording at the same time.

## Principles

- **Audience first.** Anchor in the viewer’s real decision, comparison, misunderstanding, noticing, or task — not an analyst abstraction.
- **One connected story.** Seven scenes are one Short, not seven related facts and not a research paraphrase.
- **Story lens.** Prefer the Atom’s strongest useful way of seeing the subject over the most generic explanation. Do not invent a marketing angle the Atom does not support.
- **Dance, not list.** Connect beats with consequence or contrast (therefore / but behavior). Answering an open question must open the next; only Scene 7 closes clean. Do not write an “and then” fact sequence.
- **Write toward the ending.** Decide the payoff before Scene 1 so Scene 7 can resolve the opening. The payoff must pass a retell test: one two-beat line — setup, then turn — a viewer could repeat to a friend tomorrow, using none of the Atom's nouns. Remove unnecessary payoff hedging. Make the ending decisive, but never strengthen the Atom’s claim. Prefer a direct rule over phrases such as “can actually” when the qualification adds no truth. The second payoff beat must add a new move, consequence, or decision — not merely restate the first beat. If both beats communicate the same idea, compress them. Noun repeat is allowed when it keeps the claim clear. This is not a replay-loop last-line trick.
- **Spoken first-hearing language.** The Atom gives facts, not wording — rebuild every spoken line from scratch. Preserve the claim — never the sentence, and never the Atom's name for a thing: call each recurring thing by what the viewer sees or does with it, not by a compound noun. Write `openingQuestion`, `payoff`, beat jobs, and `hookWhy` as you would say them. Narration: one new idea, at most two sentences and 22 words. A definition never travels with its implication. A fact appears only in the scene where it changes what the viewer understands. Frame as a realization unfolding, not a verdict announced. Avoid “X is not a Y”, “X is the unit/measure/term that…”, passive verdicts, and three-technical-noun clauses. Internal planning may use precise analytical concepts; spoken story must translate them. In spoken fields, avoid like-for-like, like with like, on that basis, fair basis — say the consequence in ordinary words. Ordinary words first. Translate Atom research phrasing — do not recite it. If a technical term is required, explain it in the same breath. Speak to one viewer. Not a children’s-show voice. Not a label-instructor voice. No banned-word lists. Formal→spoken rewrite pairs teach the move across domains (travel clock, quote scope, dated accounts) — copy the transformation, never the nouns.
- **Teach through the concrete.** When a supported example makes the idea understandable, the viewer should meet that real thing early enough to learn the idea through it.
- **N / T / V do different jobs.** Narration speaks. On-screen text labels or teases. Scene description shows a concrete visual situation. Do not make all three say the same thing. Scene description is creative visual intent, not camera or generation syntax.
- **About seven seconds per scene.** One clear piece of story work. Later scenes answer prior uncertainty, reveal the next useful distinction, or move toward payoff. Vary rhythm. Do not deliver seven equally dense explanations.
- **Payoff resolves the opening.** Scene 7 answers the question, assumption, decision, tension, or promise planted in Scene 1. Do not fabricate advice beyond the Atom.
- **Truth stays closed.** Do not invent facts, examples, outcomes, customers, or drama. Do not invent product-part locations, packaging geography, or store layout the Atom does not name. If the Atom names a panel or field, point to that. Do not invent where else a number lives. If a desired beat needs unsupported information, change the treatment.

## Not these

- Seven informational cards plus a summary
- Forced emotion, first person, character, or controversy unless the Atom supports them
- Platform CTAs, hashtags, or a second Short
