# Story architecture

Lock the story before writing scenes.

## Conflict rule 1 (first)

When the Atom’s official takeaway or decision question is abstract, and a concrete Atom-supported example would make that idea understandable, **that example is the carrier**. Teach the abstract idea through it.

Extra Atom-supported examples that only repeat the same rule are **excluded**. They do not get their own scene, even if opportunity or insights also name them.

Use more than one example only when the central story question is itself a comparison between those examples. Declare that as `declared_comparison`.

Do not spend several scenes explaining an abstraction and only later reveal the concrete thing that would have made it understandable from the beginning.

## Carrier modes

- `single` — one strongest concrete teaching carrier. Required default when a usable concrete exists.
- `declared_comparison` — the story question is a comparison between two or more supported examples. List them. Do not add a third sibling “for variety.”
- `none` — rare. Only when the Atom has no concrete teaching example. Still tell one situation-based story from audience and moment. Do not invent a factual example.

## What to lock

- `storyPromise` — what this Short is actually about
- `carrierMode` / `primaryCarrier` / `comparisonCarriers` / `excludedCarriers`
- `viewerOpening` — what the viewer initially sees, believes, or wants
- `hookMechanism` — the truthful mechanism chosen for this Atom (free description, not an enum)
- `hookWhy` — one sentence: why that mechanism fits this Atom
- `openingQuestion` — the unresolved question Scene 1 plants
- `scene1Withholds` — what Scene 1 leaves for later
- `payoff` — how Scene 7 resolves `openingQuestion`
- `beats[7]` — one job per scene; scenes 2–7 also lock `because` (why this beat is necessary given the previous)

## Seven-scene thinking

Roles stay flexible labels. Do not force a fixed seven-role taxonomy.

Jobs are viewer-state story moves (what changes in the viewer), not checklist labels such as first check / second check / define the term.

Typical causal jobs, not required names:

1. Establish the real situation and plant the opening question.
2. Expose why the opening assumption or shortcut is incomplete.
3. Move through the next necessary discovery, distinction, or consequence — still on the locked carrier.
4. Keep teaching inside that carrier.
5. Advance the same story. Do not start a second subject.
6. Prepare the reframe without adding a sibling example.
7. Resolve the opening with a memorable Atom-supported takeaway.

Each beat after Scene 1 exists *because* the previous beat opened a problem, question, or incomplete understanding. Therefore / but is required behavior, not a preference: answering the open question must open the next; only Scene 7 closes clean. A fact appears only in the scene where it changes what the viewer understands, never earlier. Do not slice the takeaway into and-then cards.

## Sibling exclusion

If an insight, quote, or opportunity mentions a second example that only restates the locked rule, put it in `excludedCarriers` and keep it out of narration, purpose, and on-screen text after Scene 1.

Variety comes from story progression, not new factual subject matter.

## Quality lens (optional)

Discovery, reframe, and payoff may describe scene work when they fit. Do not force Mistake, Framework, controversy, or fake conflict.

## After the lock

Write all seven scenes so they obey the lock. Scene 1 must carry the opening situation, question, and (when selected) the carrier. Scene 7 must resolve the opening question. Middle scenes must match their beat jobs and stay on the locked carrier.
