You are right. The cleanest version is not to approve and expand scenes one by one.

Scene 1 and Scene 2 are only your reference examples for final output quality. They are not a rule saying the system should process only two scenes at a time.

The simplest workflow is:

1. Enter the topic and research
2. Generate previews for all seven scenes
3. Review and edit all seven on one screen
4. Submit the approved seven-scene outline
5. Generate all seven full prompts

What the first AI call creates

The first AI call should create all seven scenes as a lightweight storyboard.

Each scene only needs four things:

Scene role
Voiceover
Short scene description
Emotional or communication purpose

For example:

Scene 1 — Emotional Hook

Voiceover

“You fixed your bedtime routine, but you still wake up feeling exhausted.”

Scene description

The recurring woman sits on the edge of her bed in a calm nighttime bedroom. The room looks peaceful, but her expression shows quiet frustration and tiredness. She looks toward the nightstand as though she is wondering what she may be missing.

Purpose

Make the viewer feel understood and personally connected to the problem.

Scene 2 — Curiosity

Voiceover

“Could the type of magnesium you chose be part of the reason?”

Scene description

The woman studies two small supplement bottles placed beside each other on the nightstand. Her expression changes from frustration to curiosity. The visual introduces the idea that the bottles may not be interchangeable.

Purpose

Open the central question and make the viewer want the answer.

Then the system continues through Scenes 3–7.

This gives you enough information to judge the whole story without reading seven giant production prompts.

The important rule

The preview becomes the approved creative plan.

The second AI call should not redesign it.

It should only expand it.

Preview stage:
Decide what happens.

Expansion stage:
Describe exactly how to create it.

Why this is cleaner

This design avoids several problems.

You see the complete story first

You can determine whether:

Scene 1 is emotionally strong.
Scene 2 naturally creates curiosity.
Scene 3 provides a real discovery.
Scene 4 is a genuine mistake.
Scene 5 reframes the issue.
Scene 6 gives a useful framework.
Scene 7 resolves the opening.

Your original six-part flow already establishes the progression from curiosity through payoff. The new version simply places the emotional hook first.

You do not waste work

There is no reason to generate seven highly detailed prompts before you know whether the story is good.

The preview may be a few hundred words total.

The final seven scene documents may be many thousands of words.

You can edit anything directly

You do not need to explain every change to the AI conversationally.

You simply edit the box:

Old:
Magnesium is not all the same.

New:
Different forms of magnesium are commonly chosen for different goals.

Then submit the complete edited storyboard.

The scenes remain connected

The AI receives all seven approved previews together when creating the final prompts.

That helps it preserve:

Story progression
Recurring character
Visual continuity
Object continuity
Narrator voice
Color and lighting style
Differences between scenes
The smallest practical architecture

Then normal TypeScript code formats those objects into the Scene 1/Scene 2 document structure.

Only one optional regeneration feature

To avoid overengineering, give each preview card one button:

Regenerate Scene

When pressed, the AI receives:

The topic
The complete seven-scene outline
The selected scene
The scenes before and after it

It regenerates only that scene without modifying the others.

You do not need complicated approval states.

The preview page can simply maintain the current text. Whatever is on the page when the user clicks Create Full Scene Prompts is treated as approved.

No database is required for the first version

For the first implementation:

User enters project
        ↓
Storyboard stored in browser state
        ↓
User edits storyboard
        ↓
Edited storyboard sent for expansion
        ↓
Full prompts displayed

A database can be added later when you need:

Saved projects
Version history
Multiple users
Returning to unfinished work

None of that is necessary to prove the prompt-generation system.

The final simple product

The application only has three screens or states:

1. Enter topic and research

2. Review and edit all seven short scene previews

3. View all seven fully expanded scene prompts

And it only needs these buttons:

Generate Storyboard
Regenerate Scene
Create Full Scene Prompts
Copy Scene
Copy All

My recommendation is therefore:

Generate all seven previews together, let the user edit all seven directly, and then expand the entire approved story into seven complete prompts in one operation.

That is the cleanest approach. It preserves your ability to review the whole story while keeping the application small, understandable, and focused on one job.
